> The deploy succeeded — Docker just used the .env from my laptop, so production has been emailing order confirmations to my mom since Tuesday.

## Why it lands

The joke works because the failure is completely plausible — everyone who has shipped with Docker knows a stray local `.env` can silently ride along, so the setup feels true before it gets funny. The twist then shrinks a production incident to the most personal scale possible: real customers' order confirmations have been going to one person's mom, for days, unnoticed since Tuesday. That mix of technical credibility, a specific victim, and the deadpan "the deploy succeeded" framing is what makes it read as a real developer excuse rather than a generic one-liner.
